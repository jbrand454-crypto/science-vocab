Everything around us feels completely solid, but the universe is actually built from tiny, invisible building blocks, atoms. Inside, every atom has a dense center, the nucleus, surrounded by paths of traveling particles, called electrons. But standard textbook diagrams are misleading. An atom is actually 99.9% empty space. If this outer electron orbit were the size of a massive football stadium, that dense nucleus at the center would only be the size of a single marble. All the different materials in the universe, from oxygen to gold, are just unique variations of this mostly empty blueprint, called elements. Because matter is almost entirely empty space, if you packed the physical particles of your entire class together without that space, every single one of you would fit on the head of a pin.
