Every element on the periodic table has its own unique ID card. We call it the atomic number. Hydrogen's atomic number is 1. Carbon is 6 and oxygen is 8. But what do these numbers actually measure? It represents the exact count of protons inside an atom's nucleus. Hydrogen's number is 1 because it has exactly one central proton. The entire periodic table is strictly ordered by this count. As you jump from carbon's six protons up to oxygens eight, the ID bumper scales perfectly. Now you can change in atoms' neutrons to make isotopes, or change electrons to make ions. The elements identity stays the same. But protons act as a permanent identity lock. Add or remove a single proton and that lock breaks. You've created a completely different element. No two elements will ever share the same atomic number. It's each element's unique, unchangeable fingerprint.
