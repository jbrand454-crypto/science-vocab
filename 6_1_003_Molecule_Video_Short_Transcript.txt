When individual atoms stick together, something incredible happens. They form molecules. The way these tiny building blocks connect determines the properties of every material in our world. Take water, for example. It starts with two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. When they chemically bond together, they don't just mix. They lock into a precise bent shape to create H2O. Together, they transform into a completely new substance with unique properties, like surface tension. But molecules aren't only made of different elements. Oxygen gas is simpler, just two identical oxygen atoms bonded. Whether mixed or matching, a molecule is the absolute smallest unit of a compound that still holds on to that compound's unique chemical properties. Break this bond, and it's no longer water. This single unit is the baseline. Now, zoom out. A single glass of water contains more molecules than stars in the observable universe. [BLANK_AUDIO]
