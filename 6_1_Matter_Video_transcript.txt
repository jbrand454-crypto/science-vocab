Everything around you is matter, but what exactly makes something matter? Look at this heavy wooden desk, and then look at the completely empty space right above it. To be matter, an object must be made of actual physical stuff. Particles. We call the total amount of that stuff, it's mass. It also has to take up three-dimensional space, regardless of its shape. That footprint is its volume. Inside that solid desk, the particles are packed tightly together, locked in place, ad heat, and they loosen up into a liquid, sliding past each other, add more heat, and they break apart entirely, flying around as a gas. So think about an ice cube melting into a puddle. Its shape is entirely different, and its volume changes. But because no actual particles were added or taken away, the total amount of stuff is identical. This unbreakable rule is called the conservation of mass, which brings us back to that supposedly empty space hovering right above the desk. It isn't empty at all. Air counts as matter too. Even though you can't see it with the naked eye, it still has mass and it definitely takes up space.
